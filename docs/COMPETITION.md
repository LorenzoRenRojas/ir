# Competitive Landscape & Positioning — IR

_Last updated: July 2026. Sourced from public pricing/feature pages (see end).
This is the honest internal read, not marketing copy. Use it to speak
confidently at APEX and to decide what to build next._

## The one-paragraph truth

IR does **not** beat GovWin at what GovWin is best at — analyst-validated
pre-RFP intelligence, sourced by 150+ human analysts from budget signals years
before a solicitation exists, with 20+ years of award history. We will not out-
analyst a company with 150 analysts. **But GovWin is the wrong fight.** GovWin
sells $12K–$40K/yr enterprise terminals to capture teams at mid-to-large
integrators. IR's buyer is the small business that can't afford GovWin and
doesn't need an analyst — they need to find winnable work, know when it's
coming, and get help responding, at a self-serve price. In that segment our
real competitors are HigherGov, GovTribe, and the new AI-native players
(GovDash, Sweetspot, Procurement Sciences). Against them our edge is a genuinely
**integrated loop** (find → score → radar → pipeline → draft → send-to-officer)
delivered with **design taste** the incumbents don't have, at a price that
undercuts everyone but the data-only tools.

## The market is really three categories

Procurement intelligence stopped being one thing. Know which fight you're in:

1. **Pre-RFP signal detection** — GovWin owns this. Budget/forecast tracking,
   analyst-written opportunity reports. Deep, expensive, human.
2. **Opportunity tracking** — HigherGov, GovTribe. Search/filter/alert over
   SAM.gov + award history. Affordable, self-serve, data-broad.
3. **Proposal automation** — GovDash, Procurement Sciences (Awarded AI + Rogue),
   Sweetspot, GovEagle. AI that drafts compliant responses.

**IR is trying to span all three in one product.** That's the bet — and it's
either the whole thesis or a fatal case of spreading thin. The discipline: be
category-2 excellent today (we mostly are), ship a real category-3 feature
(the AI proposal engine — needs ANTHROPIC_API_KEY), and treat category-1 as a
"lite" differentiator (Recompete Radar), not a claim to out-analyst GovWin.

## Top 5 competitors

### 1. GovWin IQ (Deltek) — the incumbent, the wrong fight
- **Price:** custom, typically **$12K–$40K/yr**, up to ~$119K. Avg deal ~$29K.
- **Strength:** 150+ analysts, "Dela" AI, forecast/budget intel years pre-RFP,
  20+ yr history, teaming database. The category's gold standard.
- **Weakness:** overpriced for most, sales-gated, annual contracts, intel-only
  (no proposal help), enterprise-clunky UX.
- **How we win:** don't compete on their turf. Win the buyer they price out —
  small business — on price, self-serve onboarding, and the fact that we help
  you *respond*, not just *find*.

### 2. HigherGov — our closest direct competitor (now part of Procurement Sciences)
- **UPDATE (May 2026): HigherGov was acquired by Procurement Sciences** —
  verified via the Procurement Sciences announcement blog. PSci now owns
  Awarded AI + Rogue AI + HigherGov: data, AI proposals, and search under one
  enterprise-oriented roof. Expect HigherGov's roadmap, pricing, and sales
  motion to drift upmarket toward PSci's capture-team buyer.
- **What this means for IR:** the value-leader independent in our segment just
  got consolidated. That makes **"the independent tool for the small shop"**
  IR's truthful new positioning — every other credible option is now either
  enterprise (GovWin), sales-gated AI (GovDash), or part of the PSci
  conglomerate. Say it at APEX: we're not owned by anyone selling upmarket;
  the small contractor IS the business, not the entry tier of one.
- **Price:** **$500 / $2,500 / $5,000 per year** (free trial, no free tier) —
  pre-acquisition pricing; watch for changes under PSci.
- **Strength:** the value leader. 65M+ prime/sub awards to 2000, contact &
  incumbent research, grants, **recompete identification built in**, SLED data.
  Broad, cheap, trusted by small businesses.
- **Weakness:** it's a data/search tool. No profile-learned scoring, no proposal
  help, no send-to-officer, utilitarian UX.
- **Reality check:** **HigherGov already has recompete tracking.** Our Recompete
  Radar is NOT a category-of-one — never claim it is. Our edge is that ours is
  *scored to the user*, *pushed via alerts*, *wired into the pipeline*, and
  included in the price — not a filter you remember to run.

### 3. GovTribe — the affordable pipeline pick
- **Price:** **$1,350/yr** federal, **$1,800/yr** fed+state.
- **Strength:** built by ex-contractors, intuitive, ML recommendations, light
  pipeline. The "GovWin was too much" choice.
- **Weakness:** federal-centric, no proposal automation, thin on personalization.
- **How we win:** deeper personalization (Voyage learning), the response layer,
  and better design.

### 4. GovDash — the AI-native threat we should actually watch
- **Price:** not public; sales-led. YC W22, NY/Arlington.
- **Strength:** full lifecycle — Find, Capture, Proposal, Pricer, Contract +
  "Dash" agent. GovCon-trained models, FAR-aware, drafts proposals 50–60%
  faster. Well-funded, moving fast, same small-to-mid buyer as us.
- **Weakness:** no public CMMC L2 / SOC 2, FedRAMP *equivalency* (not full),
  Azure OpenAI lock-in, **4–5 week onboarding**, sales-gated.
- **How we win (if we move fast):** self-serve vs 5-week onboarding; one clean
  price vs enterprise quote; design. But **their proposal engine is real and
  ours isn't built yet** — this is the gap that matters most.

### 5. Procurement Sciences (Awarded AI) + Sweetspot / GovEagle — the AI pack
- **Procurement Sciences:** end-to-end "Awarded AI," acquired Rogue AI (Feb
  2026) **and HigherGov (May 2026)** — now the consolidator of the mid-market.
  Enterprise, capture-team focused; the acquisitions point upmarket, away from
  the sub-$500/yr small-shop buyer.
- **Sweetspot / GovEagle / LotusPetal / BidWritePro:** AI proposal tools aimed
  squarely at small-business federal contractors — our exact segment.
- **How we win:** we're not just a proposal tool bolted onto search — we're the
  whole loop. But we have to *ship* the proposal engine to make that real.

## Where IR genuinely wins today (defensible, say these at APEX)

1. **The integrated loop at one self-serve price.** Nobody at $79–199/mo does
   find → score → radar → pipeline → draft → send-to-officer in one clean tool.
   The others make you stitch 2–3 subscriptions together.
2. **Design & UX.** The incumbents are dense and dated. IR is genuinely
   beautiful. For the self-serve small-business buyer, this closes deals.
3. **Personalization that learns.** The Voyage embedding layer (preference
   vector updated on every save/win) is more sophisticated than filter-based
   competitors — closer to "Facebook feed for contracts" than "saved search."
4. **Send-to-contracting-officer.** Genuinely uncommon. One click, replies route
   to the user. A concrete "wow" in a demo.
5. **Recompete Radar, scored & included.** Not unique (HigherGov has recompetes),
   but ours is scored-to-you + alerting + pipeline-wired + included — framed
   that way, it's a real edge, not a false claim.

## Where IR is behind — the honest gap list

1. **Proposal engine isn't built.** The AI players' core capability, and it's on
   our own landing page as a promise. This is the #1 thing to ship. Needs
   ANTHROPIC_API_KEY. Until then, "proposal drafts" means template-driven.
2. **Data depth.** 45-day SAM window + live USAspending queries vs HigherGov's
   65M awards to 2000. We look thin on historical/incumbent research.
3. **No security posture.** No SOC 2, no CMMC. GovDash gets dinged for lacking
   these — and we lack them too. For a buyer handling sensitive capture data,
   this is a trust gap. Near-term answer: an honest "SOC 2 in progress" roadmap
   on /security; longer-term, actually pursue it once there's revenue.
4. **No SLED (state/local).** HigherGov, GovTribe, Jorpex have it. Federal-only
   caps our addressable market.
5. **Solo founder, no analyst moat.** We can't manufacture GovWin's content moat.
   Our moat has to be product + design + the learning loop + price.

## The play, in priority order

1. **Ship the AI proposal engine** (ANTHROPIC_API_KEY). It converts "proposal
   drafts" from a promise into our category-3 answer and directly counters the
   GovDash/Sweetspot pack. Highest ROI feature we can build.
2. **Bid/No-Bid scorecard + win/loss learning loop** (already scoped). Cheap,
   deepens the personalization story APEX advisors respect.
3. **Honest security roadmap** on /security (SOC 2 in progress). Closes the
   trust gap cheaply and truthfully.
4. **Never claim category-of-one on recompetes.** Positioning is price-tier +
   integration + scoring, not existence. (Enforced in HANDBOOK.)
5. **Deepen data** where cheap: lengthen the SAM window and cache more award
   history so incumbent research stops looking thin.
